## Problem

Inflation answers how prices changed over time, while cost-of-living data answers how prices differ
between places. Neither question alone can translate the purchasing power of a dollar amount from
one date and location to another.

## Approach

COLA Maps keeps the two adjustments separate and multiplies them:

1. A time coefficient comes from a selected national price series, stored as source levels and
   rebased to a user-chosen anchor date (default January 20, 1995). Official CPI-U history reaches
   back to January 1913; other series begin when their source publishes them.
2. A location coefficient comes from the ratio between destination and origin regional price
   levels.

The main converter defaults to the Bureau of Economic Analysis 2024 metropolitan-area Regional
Price Parities for its location adjustment. The Map tab also exposes county estimates, rent
measures, and place-level proxies, with each location labeled by the data vintage it uses.

The underlying workbook and JSON export are generated by Python. The installable Progressive Web
App is built with React, TypeScript, Vite, and ECharts. It caches the inflation series during
installation and caches larger map layers as they are opened. Anchor date and household-basket
weights stay in that browser’s `localStorage`.

## Result

The app provides an inflation explorer with a movable chart anchor, a regional coefficient map
that can be searched and inspected, configurable household-basket weights, and a converter that
accepts an amount, origin date and metro, and destination date and metro across each series’
full available range. Its Sources tab links each dataset, defines its acronyms, and states the
reliability and limitations of the measurements.

## Limitations

Regional coefficients are cross-sectional snapshots rather than historical local price series.
Metro purchasing-power conversions use 2024 data even when the selected dates are earlier. County
and place layers are exploratory and should be compared only within the same geographic grain.
